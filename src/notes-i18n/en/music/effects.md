# List of Musical Effects

Here is a structured list of the main musical effects, grouped by purpose. This overview covers both traditional studio processors and creative sound-design tools.

## 🎛️ Dynamic Processing

These effects control the loudness and dynamic range of a signal, making the sound denser, more even, or, conversely, more expressive.

- **Compressor**: Reduces the difference between the loudest and quietest parts of a signal, making the sound denser and more "glued".
- **Limiter**: A type of compressor with a very high compression ratio. Used to prevent overload and suppress short peaks.
- **Gate / Expander**: A gate mutes or strongly attenuates a signal when its level drops below a set threshold. An expander, by contrast, increases the dynamic range, making quiet sounds even quieter.
- **De-esser**: A specialized compressor that selectively suppresses harsh, hissing sounds (sibilants) in a vocal part.
- **Transient Designer**: Lets you independently boost or reduce the attack (initial impulse) and sustain (tail) of a sound, making it more "punchy" or smoother.

## 🌊 Modulation Effects

These effects are based on modulating (changing) one or more sound parameters over time, giving the sound movement, depth and character.

- **Chorus**: Creates the illusion of several instruments or voices sounding at once by adding copies of the original signal with small, varying time shifts. This makes the sound richer and fuller.
- **Flanger**: Similar to a chorus, but uses a shorter delay (5–15 ms) and feedback. Creates a characteristic "whistling" or "jet-like" effect reminiscent of an airplane taking off.
- **Phaser**: Unlike a flanger, it works not with delay time but with the phase of the signal. Creates a "swirling", pulsating effect that seems to "scan" the sound.
- **Tremolo**: A fast, regular change in the loudness (amplitude) of a signal. Creates a pulsating effect.
- **Vibrato**: A regular change in the pitch (frequency) of a signal. Gives the sound a "trembling" or wavering character.
- **Auto-Wah**: An effect that mimics the sound of a wah-wah pedal but is controlled not by the musician's foot, but by the input signal level. Playing louder opens the effect further.

## ⏱️ Delay and Echo Effects

- **Delay / Echo**: Creates clear, repeating copies of the original signal at a set time interval. Can be used for rhythmic repeats or simply to give the sound spatial depth.
- **Multitap Delay**: A type of delay that creates several repeats, each with its own delay time, level and pan position.

## 🏛️ Reverb (Reverberation)

This is arguably the most important effect for creating a sense of space. It simulates the acoustic reflections of sound from the walls of a room, creating a sense of volume.

- **Types of reverb**: There are many algorithms simulating different spaces: **room (Room)**, **hall (Hall)**, **plate (Plate)** (emulation of old plate reverbs), **chamber (Chamber)** and others.
- **Early Reflections**: A separate type of processing that adds only the very first, clearest reflections from the walls. This helps "glue" a sound to a specific place in space without adding a long "tail".

## 🔥 Distortion and Saturation

These effects add nonlinear distortion to the sound, enriching it with additional harmonics.

- **Distortion / Overdrive**: Create a "dirty", overdriven sound. Overdrive is usually softer and more musical, while distortion is more aggressive. Widely used for electric guitar.
- **Fuzz**: An extreme form of distortion that turns the signal almost into a square wave, giving it a "buzzing" and very dense character.
- **Saturation**: Soft, "analog" distortion that simulates the saturation of magnetic tape or valve equipment. Makes the sound warmer, denser and more "gluey".
- **Bit-Crusher**: An effect that deliberately reduces the bit depth and/or sample rate of a signal. Creates a "retro", "lo-fi" or digital, robotic sound.

## 🎚️ Filtering and Equalization

- **EQ / Equalizer**: The main tool for correcting frequency balance. Lets you boost or attenuate specific frequency ranges (low, mid, high).
- **Filters**: Devices that cut off frequencies above (Low-Pass Filter) or below (High-Pass Filter) a certain cutoff point. Often used in electronic music to create "opening" and "closing" effects on a sound.

## 🎼 Pitch and Harmony

- **Pitch Shifter**: Changes the pitch of a signal without changing its duration. Can be used to create harmonies, a "chipmunk" effect, or more radical transformations.
- **Harmonizer**: A type of pitch shifter that automatically adds harmonic intervals (e.g. a third or a fifth) to the original sound, creating the illusion of choral singing or several instruments playing.
- **Vocoder**: Analyzes the spectrum of one signal (e.g. a voice) and transfers its characteristics to another signal (e.g. a synthesizer). Allows a synthesizer to "speak" with a human voice.

## 🎧 Spatial and Stereo Effects

- **Panning**: Placing a sound in the stereo field (left, right, center).
- **Stereo Widening**: Techniques that make the stereo image wider than it actually is by manipulating phase or the difference between channels.

## ✨ Special and Creative Effects

- **Ring Modulator**: Multiplies two signals (e.g. your instrument and an internal oscillator) by each other. Creates metallic, "otherworldly" and dissonant timbres.
- **Lo-Fi**: An umbrella term for effects that deliberately degrade audio quality, adding noise, "dirt", artifacts and limiting the frequency range.
- **Shimmer Reverb**: A special kind of reverb in which the "tail" of the effect is processed by a pitch shifter (usually an octave up). Creates a huge, ethereal, "angelic" sound.

This list is not exhaustive, but it covers the vast majority of effects you will encounter when working with sound. If you would like to explore a particular effect or its applications in more detail, just ask.
