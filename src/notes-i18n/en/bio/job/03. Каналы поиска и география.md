## Rejected directions

By the author's decision of 24 November 2026, the following are not considered:

- European Union;
- Georgia;
- Armenia;
- Turkey;
- UAE;
- Kazakhstan;
- Serbia.

Old links to local job boards in these countries (`findjobs.ge`, `jobs.ita.am`, `hirify.me`, etc.) are removed from the search plan.

## Channels that remain

### Russian remote work — fast, stable income in RUB

- `hh.ru` — filter "Can work remotely"; queries: JavaScript, Frontend, Fullstack, Node.js, DevOps, Linux, administrator.
- `career.habr.com/vacancies/remote` — around 470 vacancies at the time of analysis; it's worth polishing the profile and CV.
- SuperJob and thematic Telegram job channels for JS/DevOps.
- Search phrasings: not just "developer", but also "operations engineer", "developer support", "system administrator + automation".

### Global remote work

- LinkedIn — the main channel; filters `Remote`, `Anywhere`, `Europe`, `Worldwide`; check the phrasings `visa sponsorship`, `relocation support`, `EOR`.
- Wellfound — startups that value T-shaped profiles.
- We Work Remotely, Remote OK, Arc.dev, Remotive, Jobicy.
- Aggregators such as Jaabz, Remocate, Relomote, Stafka should only be used as a source of leads: lots of noise and hiring restrictions by country.

### Direct outreach — the best channel for a non-standard profile

Target segments:

- music-tech: web players, streaming, radio, DAWs, sound design tools;
- self-hosted and open-source products;
- wiki / knowledge-management tools;
- small product teams where one engineer covers frontend + infra + audio.

Here `player.js` and typo-tolerant search work better than an abstract "I know React".

## Important payment constraint

According to open reviews from 2026, standard payment rails to Russian accounts often don't work: Wise, PayPal, Payoneer, Stripe and most freelance platforms restrict the Russian Federation. So at an early interview it's worth asking:

- Can you hire contractors based in Russia?
- What payment rails do you use for Russia?
- Do you have an EOR or local entity in the target country?

Without answers to these questions, relocation can't be planned: offers with foreign payment often fall apart at the last step.

## Open question: the new region

Until a relocation direction is chosen, the search plan remains incomplete. Groups not rejected so far:

- East Asia: Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, China;
- Southeast Asia: Thailand, Malaysia, Vietnam, Indonesia, Philippines, Singapore;
- Latin America: Mexico, Brazil, Argentina, Chile, Colombia, Uruguay, Paraguay, Costa Rica;
- Middle East excluding the UAE: Israel, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, Bahrain, Oman;
- Africa: South Africa, Kenya, Morocco;
- Oceania: Australia, New Zealand;
- Not formally EU but Europe-oriented: the UK, Switzerland, Norway, Iceland — check visa and banking rules separately.

The safest phrasing for the profile until a decision is made: `Remote from Russia (UTC+3), open to relocation to a non-EU country`.

## Check before applying

- Does this vacancy accept candidates from the Russian Federation, or at least remote candidates in the UTC+3 time zone?
- Is there any wording about pay, taxes and legal arrangements?
- If relocating: who is the employer — a local company, an EOR or the parent company?
- Does the anchor role match the job title? If not, apply only with a very strong cover letter.
