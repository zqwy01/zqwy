Privatization is when private companies steal the achievements of specific people that are held in the ownership of the state.

Nationalization is when the state steals the achievements of specific people that are held in the ownership of a private company.

Socialization is when society steals the achievements of specific people held in independent ownership on the basis of their natural right to copyright and declares them a public achievement. Even if the author did not declare them publicly available, the collective always strives to devalue the unique, proceeding from an imagined justice of equality.

In sum, this defines a hybrid form of discrimination.
In all three cases, a person's copyright is worth exactly nothing and, at the same time, everything.

This does not mean that I support this. On the contrary, I wrote about reality rather than smoothing it over with useless fantasies about beauty.

Because for any sober person it is important not to refuse to become part of systems:
- states;
- corporations;
- societies.

Consequently: I have formulated a dilemma about defining such a person who can sincerely be part of a system, not merely on the basis of what is predetermined for them. After all, they cannot choose it not because it does not align with an ideological interest, but because it does not align with a person's large-scale interest.

Is there a definite position on this concept?

Given that it is impossible to assign a personal policeman to every person, but it is possible to assign AI and make people use at least a phone, or at most track every person's actions by satellite, one is left with an extreme but significant conclusion: this reasoning is a bridge between ideologies. It is a practical centrism in which one invents one's own outcome.
