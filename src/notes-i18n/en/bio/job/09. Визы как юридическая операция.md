The author's clarification: merely listing visas ("H-1B, EB-2, O-1") doesn't exhaust the question. The point isn't the list, but that **every visa is a legal construct with its own evidentiary procedure**. It's not enough to "possess an achievement" — you have to perform a specific legal operation: translate real facts into the language of the statute.

Below is how this works using the USA as an example, and what material already available can be used.

## How the legal operation works

USCIS explicitly distinguishes the **standard** from the **evidentiary mechanism** ([Policy Manual, Volume 2, Part M, Chapter 4](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-2-part-m-chapter-4)):

> «The evidentiary requirements are not the standard for the classification, but are instead the mechanism for establishing whether the standard is met.»

So meeting three criteria doesn't automatically grant the visa. The officer first checks the mechanical part (3 documentary criteria from the list), then assesses the **totality of evidence**: is there "sustained national or international recognition" and placement in the "small percentage of the best in the field"?

The operation therefore has two steps:

1. **Qualifying the evidence** — fitting the facts to the wording of the criteria.
2. **Translation into the standard** — showing that these facts together mean recognition, not merely activity.

### Comparable evidence — the most important tool

If a criterion "does not easily apply to the profession", it may be replaced with comparable evidence. The official example: for someone from industry rather than academia, presenting at a major industry show can be comparable to publishing a scholarly paper; for an entrepreneur, a stake in a startup is comparable to a high salary. You have to explain **why the criterion does not apply** and **why the proposed evidence is comparable** — a bare assertion doesn't work, but a detailed and plausible explanation can be accepted as sufficient.

That is the "legal operation" in its pure form: not pretending to be someone else, but proving that your real activity has comparable weight.

## Visa map by type of operation

| Visa | Key operation | What must be proven | Realistic for a Russian citizen with 2 years of experience |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H-1B** | Find an employer-sponsor and win the lottery | Specialty occupation, qualifications, LCA | Yes, but lottery + employer dependence |
| **L-1A/L-1B** | Work for a year at a foreign branch, then transfer | Relationship between legal entities, specialized knowledge/management | Yes, if the employer is international |
| **F-1 → OPT → H-1B** | Pay for studies, then obtain work status | University admission, money, studies | Yes, but expensive and slow |
| **J-1** | Exchange programme | Host organization | Yes, often as a bridge |
| **O-1A** | Assemble a case on 3 of 8 criteria + advisory opinion + totality | Recognition in the field | Difficult: a public trail is needed |
| **O-1B (arts)** | Prove "distinction" in the arts | Leading roles, reviews, publications | Theoretically (music/video), but tough |
| **EB-2 NIW** | Two operations: EB-2 baseline + Dhanasar test | Specialty + national importance + "well positioned" | Difficult: with 2 years there's no 5-year base or recognition |
| **EB-1A** | 3 of 10 criteria, higher threshold | Verdict of "top of the field" | Unrealistic now |
| **EB-3** | Employer + PERM | Job offer, absence of local candidates | Slow |
| **EB-5** | Investment | Capital | Not relevant |
| **E-2** | Investment + citizenship of a treaty country | Citizenship of a treaty party | **Structurally closed**: Russia is not on the E-2 list |
| **E-3** | Australian citizenship + specialty occupation | Australian passport | Only available after naturalization in AU |
| **IER** | Entrepreneur parole | Investments/grants, substantial contribution | Possible with money and investors |
| **DV** | Lottery | Citizenship of a participating country | Check year by year, the odds are small |

## What is already in the repository for O-1A / NIW

Criterion 5 of O-1A ("original scientific or business contributions of major significance in the field") explicitly includes:

> «Contributions to repositories of software, data, designs, protocols, or other technical resources with evidence of significant scientific, scholarly, or business-related impact in the field.»

This is closest to the user's project: large local software, generators, a search index, Web Audio. But the keywords are **with evidence of significant impact**: a public repository, releases, third-party mentions, expert letters. The repository is already open (author's clarification, see `11`); the problem isn't privacy but the missing edge — there's no link to it in `dev/links/index.html`, so the connectedness still needs to be built up.

**A concrete operation that requires no new data from the author:**

1. Make the repository public and bring the README in line with the facts (445 entries, 40 releases, 296 tracks, baseline `23 PASS / 14 FAIL`).
2. Produce 3 public cases with a changelog and artifacts.
3. Assemble an evidence folder: screenshots, releases, issue/PR, mentions, letters from developers.
4. Obtain an **advisory opinion** in advance — a written opinion from an expert or a panel of experts, mandatory for O-1 (except when no such panel exists).
5. For NIW, formulate a specific **endeavor**, not "I work as a programmer": for example, open tools for autonomous local media archives.

## Rule instability as part of the operation

The story of the $100,000 H-1B fee shows that the procedure itself changes:

- 19.09.2025 — Proclamation 10973;
- 08.06.2026 — a federal court struck down the payment as an unlawful tax (vacatur);
- 24.07.2026 — the First Circuit declined to stay the vacatur;
- 18.09.2026 — the proclamation was extended to 21.09.2027, but the vacatur remains in force;
- as of 05.10.2026 the payment **is not collected**;
- a separate proposal for a $103,265 fee for cap-subject petitions has not taken effect.

Source: [The $100,000 H-1B Fee: Current Status and Full Timeline](https://www.immigrationworld.com/usa/100000-h-1b-fee/) (18.08.2026, updated in the text through October 2026).

So even a "known route" requires checking the status on the day of filing. That's another legal operation — monitoring the status.

## Conclusion

The author is right: the question isn't having "outstanding achievements", but **which legal operation turns existing facts into evidence**. But the operation doesn't cancel two things:

- for O-1/NIW/EB-1 the threshold is "recognition in the field", and without a public trail it can't be assembled;
- for H-1B/L-1/F-1 the operation is cheaper and clearer: you need an employer or studies, not "top of the field" status.

So the practical order is: income and a public trail first, then choose the visa operation for a specific offer.
