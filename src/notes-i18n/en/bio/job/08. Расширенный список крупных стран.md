## The most valuable new candidates

### 1. USA, northern states

Structurally this is the best missed option: a large country, English, cold winters, farms and detached houses. Cannabis remains illegal at the federal level, while the states are a patchwork: according to 2026 reviews, full legalization is absent in part of the northern states — Wisconsin, Iowa, Indiana, Idaho, Kansas, Nebraska and Wyoming are named among them (data varies; check at the time of moving). Of these, by climate and cloudiness it makes more sense to look at Wisconsin, Iowa, Indiana and Pennsylvania rather than Kansas/Nebraska/Wyoming — the prairies have more wind and sun.

The main downside is not impossibility but the difficulty and unpredictability of immigration. H-1B really does go through a lottery and requires an employer sponsor, EB-2/EB-3 take years, O-1 is aimed at outstanding achievement. There are also workarounds: studying in the USA (F-1 → OPT → H-1B), an internal transfer (L-1) after a year working in another country. So the route is real, but with "stable income" as the priority it's a high-risk path: without a sponsor and with 2 years of experience the odds are low, and the timeline stretches to years. It's more accurate to say "difficult" than "almost unattainable".

### 2. Mongolia (excluded by the author)

On climate, size, animals and the cannabis filter it passes surprisingly well: cannabis is completely banned, winters are cold, the way of life is nomadic. But land in Mongolia cannot be sold to foreigners as ownership — only leases and ownership of buildings ([Mongolian land law](https://advocate.mn/en/legal-library/land-possessors-and-land-users-in-mongolia/), [2026 review](https://www.nomadgo.info/goverment-services-mongolia/i/98685807/mongolia-foreigners-land-apartment-ownership-2026)). Plus a population of ~3.5 million and a very narrow IT job market.

### 3. Greenland — what the job market is like

Formally not the EU, an autonomous part of Denmark; area 2.16 million km², Arctic climate, cannabis banned ([Cannabis in Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannabis_in_Greenland)). Population according to the official publication [Greenland in Figures 2026](https://stat.gl/publ/en/GF/2026/pdf/Greenland%2520in%2520Figures%25202026.pdf) — about 57,000 people, the lowest population density in the world.

**Market structure according to [Nordic cooperation](https://www.norden.org/en/info-norden/job-seeking-greenland):** most jobs are in the public sector and large state-owned companies, the private sector is very small, unemployment is low, and there are shortages of doctors and some specialists. So there is a market, but it isn't a frontend/DevOps market in the usual sense.

**Who hires in IT/engineering:** the Government of Greenland (Naalakkersuisut), the telecom operator [Tusass](https://www.tusass.gl/en/career/vacancies/), utilities such as Nukissiorfiit (OT/SCADA), plus hospitals and municipalities. There are only a handful of vacancies, and some are project roles such as "telecom infrastructure project manager".

**Job boards:** [hireme.gl](https://hireme.gl/en), [aviisi.gl](https://aviisi.gl/en/jobs/), [jobindex.gl](https://www.jobindex.dk/cms/groenland?lang=en), Tusass' vacancies page.

**Paperwork:** for citizens of the Nordic countries, entry and work without a visa; for everyone else, a separate Greenlandic permit processed by the Danish Agency for Recruitment and Integration ([AKA](https://aka.gl/emner/moving-to-greenland-for-work?sc_lang=en)). Free movement within the EU does not automatically give the right to work in Greenland. There are rules on 90 days and a six-month tax test for remotes ([overview](https://www.stampednomad.com/guides/greenland)).

**Why it doesn't suit the author:** for all its area, this isn't a country for a house with land and a farm — very little land is suitable for farming, and the climate and logistics make keeping animals and running a smallholding difficult. Plus a very high cost of living, a housing shortage and no IT market. Greenland is beautiful exoticism, but not stable income.

### 4. Northern China (excluded by the author)

Heilongjiang, Jilin, Inner Mongolia — a cold climate, cannabis banned, enormous scale. But land cannot be sold to foreigners, and the language and internet restrictions seriously hinder remote work for foreign teams. Not suitable for stable foreign-currency income.

### 5. If "scale" is softened to "large by European standards"

Norway (0.385 million km²) — not the EU, cold climate, cannabis banned, you can have a house with land outside the city; but the market is small, the local language dominates everyday life, and it's very expensive. Sweden and Finland would suit the climate and the land, but they're in the EU. Iceland and Switzerland are very small in area.

## Why the list keeps narrowing

The intersection of seven conditions is rare:

1. a country comparable to Russia in size;
2. a cool climate without bright sun;
3. you can buy a house with land and keep animals;
4. no federal legalization of cannabis;
5. English or a closely related language;
6. legal immigration from Russia;
7. stable foreign-currency income.

So "Canada, Australia and the UK are left" is not laziness in searching, but the result of the intersection. Canada drops out on point 4, the UK on points 1 and 3, Australia passes almost everything but loses on distance and visas. The USA passes 1–5 but fails point 6.

## What the author needs to decide

To stop the list from narrowing, one of three things must be relaxed:

- **Country scale** — then Norway, New Zealand and the UK return as places to live.
- **English language** — then China, Brazil, Argentina and Chile return.
- **Immigration feasibility** — then the USA returns to the list.

A fourth option is to keep the filters as they are and earn income in Russia, postponing the move.
